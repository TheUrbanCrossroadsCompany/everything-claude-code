> **Educational content only.** This is a practical home guide, not medical or safety
> advice. For questions about chemical exposure and your health, talk to your doctor.

"Forever chemicals" (PFAS) are synthetic compounds that resist breaking down — in the
environment and in us. You can't avoid them completely, but a handful of swaps cover most
household exposure. Here they are, highest leverage first.

## 1. Filter your drinking water

Water is the single biggest lever for most households. Filters certified to reduce PFAS
(look for NSF/ANSI 53 or 58 certification) — including many reverse-osmosis and
activated-carbon systems — can cut PFAS along with heavy metals. Our tracker's water pillar
assumes filtered water for exactly this reason.

## 2. Retire scratched nonstick cookware

Traditional nonstick coatings are a classic PFAS source, especially once scratched. Cast
iron, carbon steel, and stainless are effectively forever cookware — without the forever
chemicals.

## 3. Stop heating food in plastic

Heat accelerates the migration of plastic compounds into food. Move leftovers to glass or
ceramic before microwaving. This one costs nothing.

## 4. Swap plastic cutting boards for wood

Plastic boards shed microplastics directly into food as they wear. Wooden boards are
gentler on knives anyway.

## 5. Check food packaging habits

Grease-resistant wrappers (fast food, microwave popcorn bags) have historically used PFAS
coatings. Cooking at home from whole ingredients — pillar two — sidesteps most of it.

## 6. Look at waterproofed fabrics

"Stain-resistant" and "waterproof" treatments on furniture, carpet, and clothing are a
common PFAS category. You don't need to throw things out; just factor it into future buys.

## 7. Ventilate when you cook

Simple but underrated: a range hood or open window reduces airborne exposure from cooking
at high heat, whatever your cookware.
