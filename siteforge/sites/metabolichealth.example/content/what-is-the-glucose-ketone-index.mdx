> ratio discussed in research settings — it is not a diagnostic tool or a treatment target.
> Talk to your doctor before changing how you eat or measure anything.

## The one-sentence version

The **Glucose-Ketone Index (GKI)** is a single number that compares how much glucose is in
your blood to how much of the ketone body beta-hydroxybutyrate is there, giving a rough
snapshot of which fuel your body is leaning on.

## How the math works

1. Take your blood glucose reading in mg/dL and divide it by 18 to convert it to mmol/L.
2. Divide that result by your blood ketone reading (already in mmol/L).

That's it. For example, a glucose of 90 mg/dL (which is 5.0 mmol/L) with ketones of 1.0
mmol/L gives a GKI of 5.0.

## Why people track it

Researchers studying metabolic health popularized the GKI as a simpler way to talk about
metabolic state than juggling two separate numbers. A lower GKI generally reflects lower
glucose and higher ketones — the pattern seen during fasting or very-low-carb eating. A
higher GKI reflects the typical glucose-fueled state most people are in most of the time.

What a "good" number looks like depends entirely on context, goals, and — most importantly —
what your healthcare team says. This site reports zones as informational labels only.
